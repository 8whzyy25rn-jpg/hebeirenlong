What a fitting quotation needs before a price becomes useful.

A line can look complete and still miss the one detail that changes material, inspection, packing or the commercial basis.

Price the item, not the category name.

“90-degree elbow” is not a complete buying description. Two buyers can use the same three words and need different dimensions, standards, material grades, tests and packing. An itemized quotation only works when both sides are pricing the same thing.

The fastest quotation is not the one written first. It is the one that does not need to be rebuilt after the specification is clarified.

Use one line per deliverable item.

Where sizes, pressure classes, material grades or end types change, create a new line. Do not merge them into a single “fitting package” line if the buyer expects those differences to remain visible.

Line 01 · 200 pcs · 90° long-radius elbow · 12 in · ASME B16.9 · ASTM A234 WPB · matching pipe schedule to be confirmed · marking and inspection to be confirmed

The example is not a finished specification. It makes the gaps visible: the pipe schedule, marking and inspection requirements still need a decision.

The fields that change the price.

  • Product formElbow, tee, reducer, cap, flange, pipe or forged fitting.
  • Size and geometryNPS or DN, angle, long or short radius, reducing size, face type or end type.
  • Governing standardThe project standard, ASME or EN reference, plus any project specification that changes the normal scope.
  • Material gradeState the grade on each line. Do not assume one grade across a mixed package.
  • Quantity and unitPieces, metres or sets. A total weight or package amount is not a substitute.
  • Inspection and documentsRequested test reports, marking, third-party inspection or review requirements.
  • Packing and destinationExport packing, delivery port and any project-specific loading restriction.
  • Commercial basisThe requested Incoterm, currency, delivery expectation and price validity.

Say how the fitting should relate to the pipe.

If the pipe is ordered to a schedule, do not leave the buyer and supplier to assume the fitting will match it. State whether the fitting must follow the pipe schedule, a separate fitting thickness requirement or an approved drawing.

Separate the standard from the project requirement.

A standard defines a common product requirement. A project may add marking, inspection, documentation or packing instructions on top. Both belong in a useful inquiry because a compliant standard item can still be wrong for the purchase order.

Make the destination part of the quotation.

Product cost alone does not establish a usable price. The quotation needs the destination or port, the requested Incoterm and the packing assumption. Without those fields, the supplier cannot present a consistent commercial scope.

The ordered standard, approved drawing and purchase specification govern the final product. This note is a line-list check, not a substitute for those documents.
